# Playlist lessons: fourteen episodes of Ben Marriott's Best Motion Design

Synthesis of `videos/01-*.md` through `videos/14-*.md`, covering roughly 200 showcased
pieces from 2019 to 2023, read against `twelve-principles.md`, `motion-rules.md`,
`kinetic-type.md`, `character-motion.md`, `grading-rubric.md` and `sound-design.md`,
and against the HyperFrames rule set at
`<skills>/hyperframes-animation/rules-index.md`.

Citation convention: `[ep NN]` is the episode record; a piece is named by its
burned-in corner credit, and where the record gives a screen or cell number it is
carried over as `#N`. "SEEN" claims come from the contact-sheet vision passes in the
episode records; "SAID" claims are quoted narration. Where the two disagree the
record says so and this document follows the record.

A note on what this playlist is. It is a curator talking over other people's finished
work. It is not a tutorial series. Across 14 episodes there is exactly one After
Effects graph editor on screen and it is drawn, not captured (DeeKay Kwon's illustrated
AE window, `[ep 10 #599]`). That shapes everything below: this material is strong
evidence about what reads as professional and weak evidence about how curves were
built. Where the two research strands disagree, that asymmetry is usually the reason.

## What professional looks like

### Colour is decided before anything moves

This is the single most consistent property of the praised work, and it is what the
narrator reaches for first when he explains why something is good. He states it
directly at least nine times: "a very minimal color approach each scene only has three
colors so you can concentrate on the forms" (Nerdo, Tencent China Literature,
`[ep 01 #36-#37]`); "they really master light in a limited color palette"
(The Lonely Orbit, `[ep 03 S180-S184]`); "i really love the limited color palette and
how they use light within that palette" (`[ep 06 #350]`); "with a limited color palette
and beautiful use of shadows" (Vanitas, `[ep 10 #210-#214]`).

The measured spread across the pieces the episode records characterise:

| Piece | Palette |
| --- | --- |
| Joel Plosz, The Number Thirteen `[ep 03, 07]` | two values, every midtone a dither pattern |
| Jonathan Djob Nkondo, The New Exhibition `[ep 03, 07]` | white plus three greys |
| Mehdi Alibeygi, No.7 `[ep 08, 14]` | grey, black, white |
| RuffMercy, Lament for Emmett Till `[ep 10 #299-#333]` | two values, one blue accident |
| The Lonely Orbit `[ep 03, 06]` | two blues, off-white, one orange reserved for skin |
| Gabriele Calvi, Vanitas `[ep 10 #205-#241]` | black, cream, gold, violet, one red |
| Natalie Labarre, Away `[ep 10 #268-#298]` | four flats plus paper grain |
| Eido, Rosa Parks `[ep 08, 14]` | cream, black, coral |
| BUCK, Apeel `[ep 07 #150-#176]` | cream, one fruit colour, one green |
| Illustration for Motion `[ep 13 F116]` | cream, navy, magenta, teal, sage, yellow, stop |

The exceptions prove the same rule by construction. Ordinary Folk's 2019 reel is
full-spectrum but opens on roughly four seconds of pure greyscale before any colour
arrives `[ep 13 F62-F66]`, so the chroma lands as an event. Can Drugs Take The Place
of Religion? is saturated everywhere, but the saturation is one continuous gradient
field rather than many separate hues on separate objects `[ep 08 #157-#174]`, which
is why the narrator says the colours "bleed into one another".

Where colour changes, it changes as a narrative event. Giant Ant's Hilda titles run
three colour schemes, one per theme of the feature `[ep 02 S264-S267]`. Studio AKA's
Bellefaire runs one hue per act, olive then orange then red `[ep 09 #301-#346]`.
Tomorrow's On Fire drives the field from orange to dark red under a growing smoke
plume `[ep 14 #372-#376]`.

### The transition is the shot

Ranked by how often it is the stated reason a piece is in the video, transition craft
beats everything except colour. Twelve of fourteen episodes contain an explicit
statement.

The strongest version of the claim: "they could have just gone for a very easy cut
between each sequence and that would have been fine but the effort to make them flow
together nicely into one another really sets us apart" (The Living System,
`[ep 04 S152-S162]`). The second strongest is an award given entirely for editing:
Romain Loubersanes takes Best Showreel 2020 because "every shot even cutting between
projects feels like it's a match cut where their movement flows continuously from one
shot to another that is really difficult to do" `[ep 08 #99-#107]`.

Four mechanisms recur.

**Object hand-off.** One element survives the cut and belongs to both scenes.
Ordinary Folk's Webflow sphere approaches a glowing line, the line opens into a
diamond aperture, the sphere passes through and lands inside a glass cube
`[ep 02 S103-S111]`. TEDx Sydney REAL passes a circle from a phone screen to a sphere
to a torus portal across ten different studios `[ep 07 #5, #13, #86, #92-#93]`.
Apeel dissolves an apple to a dot, grows concentric rings around it, and returns to
the apple `[ep 07 #152-#154]`. Far Cry 6 turns a smoke ring into a tyre into a camera
lens `[ep 12 #298-#304]`.

**Shape match at the same position and scale.** Away cuts a wristwatch face to a
full-frame clock, and a ballot grid rotates into a building facade
`[ep 10 #271-#272, #284-#285]`. Morgan Stanley IQ turns a cigarette pack into
factory chimneys into a fire into a coin, each morph in place `[ep 04 #302-#306]`.

**Grow-to-fill.** Alex Grigg's NaturNes takes a shape from one scene and grows it
until it becomes the ground of the next `[ep 10 #74]`. Ordinary Folk's Gospel puts a
hard-edged rectangular card carrying the incoming scene over the outgoing shot and
scales it to full bleed `[ep 06 #164-#172]`.

**Carrying motion through a cut.** Acura Type S fills the frame with a wheel passing
camera, cuts, and the smoke VFX keeps travelling in the same direction on the far side
`[ep 02 S313-S317]`. This is the only place in the playlist where the narrator
describes a velocity-matched cut in so many words.

Cross-fades are almost absent. The 2019 record states it flatly: "Transitions are
graphic, never dissolves" `[ep 13]`.

### Design carries; the animation can be small

"i think that design is the most important thing in all of animation so i love seeing
more resources dedicated to the actual design and illustration coming out not just the
animation" `[ep 13 S80-S87]`. This is his only unhedged general claim in fourteen
episodes and it is worth taking at face value.

The evidence is the award list. Bullpen's Fun and Games wins Best Design 2020 for a
piece where "the animation is really subtle just because the design and the concept is
so well" `[ep 08 #126-#130]`; the episode record describes the motion as "a slider
slides. a wheel turns. the camera dollies a few centimetres" `[ep 08]`. Gibbon
Animation's Manorism gets in on near-still oil paintings: "it is just full with these
most gorgeous background paintings with the most subtlest animation ... great animation
isn't necessarily more animation" `[ep 11 #65-#69]`. Julian Glander: "there is nothing
particularly that is so advanced about the animation but that design does all of the
heavy lifting" `[ep 10 #196-#200]`. Manon Louart takes a bat for a poster where the
flames flicker and the figure shifts weight `[ep 08 #397]`.

The corollary is that when animation is praised, it is praised for a specific named
mechanic, never for smoothness alone: the torso rotating around its own axis while the
legs keep striding (No.7, `[ep 14 T-155-T-160]`), one eye animating before the other
(design_blimp, `[ep 14 T-398-T-400]`), the neck entering frame before the body
(Murena Murena, `[ep 14 T-364-T-365]`).

### The rhythm is hold and snap, not continuous easing

The most technically precise statement in the playlist is about frame rate, not about
curves. BUCK's Mailchimp shorts "recreat[e] the textures that you would find on real
objects of that scale but also with variable frame rates and on their website they say
some of these shots have a mixture of 1 2 4 8 and even 16 frames per second in a single
shot to get that snappy pose to pose style" `[ep 07 #131-#147]`.

Amigo Total's XR piece does the same thing spatially rather than temporally: "all the
floating objects are rotating and changing color at a lower frame rate but the camera
is at a higher frame rate and it just adds so much texture to the speed of the
animation it makes it look really tactile and almost stop motion" `[ep 12 S57-S72]`.
Two layers, two cadences, one shot.

The visual consequence shows in the contact sheets. Giant Ant's Twitch spot has
genuine single-frame pose cards (cells #93, #95, #105 at `[ep 03]`, each held for
exactly one sampled second and never repeated). The MTV Christmas ident reads as
logo, gift cluster, near-empty frame, balance tower, star, scatter, tree, logo, with
each state a full stop and the transitions between them fast `[ep 07 #36-#47]`.

The narrator's word for this is "snappy", used for BUCK (`[ep 03 S325-S330]`) and for
the MTV ident (`[ep 07 #41-#45]`), and the 2021 record glosses it correctly:
"Snappy means few in-betweens, not fast" `[ep 03]`.

### Scale is the primary compositional tool, not camera movement

Three of the strongest pieces build their best moment on a size jump with no camera
move at all. The Lonely Orbit cuts full control room, single coffee mug, helmet in
extreme close-up in three shots `[ep 03 cells #213, #217, #224]`. 3:45 PM zooms from
two faces with individual crisps to a top-down field of colour blobs to a mosaic that
reads as a city grid `[ep 06 #306-#321]`. Alisha Liu's piece adds the rule the others
imply: level of detail tracks distance. "i love how when the scenes zoom out the
details just get more simplified it is really masterfully done" `[ep 06 #313-#317]`.

Bellefaire uses the same lever for meaning: "the character is just tiny in her
environment and that really helps the story and really guide your eye to be looking at
what's important" `[ep 09 Screens 219-234]`. Project Spiral gets "epic scale" out of
two colours by putting a tiny white bar with dots on the crest of a huge red band
`[ep 11 #372-#373]`.

The constant across all of them: palette and shape language stay fixed while the scale
changes, so the jump reads as scale rather than as a cut to somewhere new.

### Texture is applied late, selectively, and inside the shapes

The clearest process statement in the whole playlist is Nerdo's, and it inverts the
usual grain-as-finishing-pass habit: "they start with a very clean and clear cell rough
that they've cleaned up with solid fills then they complete with blurred edges not
every Edge just the ones that catch the light in the scene and from there grain is
applied to the blur then colors and detail a very late approach to elevate simple forms
and let the complex animation shine through" `[ep 01 #40-#43]`. The frames confirm it:
at #42 the top of the arms, the underside of the slab and the crown of the head are
softened and grainy; the outer arm contours stay hard.

The general form: "the grain isn't just a texture that's layered on top of the animation
but very controlled and intentionally used as a way to highlight certain edges and
shapes" `[ep 01 #38-#39]`.

The rest of the playlist agrees on the direction if not the precision. Minima's brush
texture "makes the thin precise strokes of these computer calculated paths feel so warm
and uneven" `[ep 02 S184-S185]`. Reece Parker's TimeLord uses "reduced palettes and
harsh shadows and everything you want to amplify and show that this was handmade"
`[ep 09 Screens 172-176]`. Illustration for Motion puts dry-brush magenta and teal
strokes inside a navy silhouette while the outer edge stays hard vector `[ep 13 F116]`.

There is a hard line drawn around faking it. Low Island's video was printed frame by
frame on a risograph and scanned back in: "we can do it reasonably well by faking it
inside after effects but really when it's done properly you can really tell"
`[ep 09 Screens 287-293]`.

### One motif, one action, one constraint

Colophon: "each of these animations is one action that loops it seems simple enough
they all start with the same colophon logo then fold like a page into another icon"
`[ep 01 #49-#50]`, and the value is inside the constraint: "the way these box tabs
overlap and fold out in a slightly offset manner creates a variation that really adds
to the visual interest" `[ep 01 #51-#52]`.

Apeel builds everything from circles and rings `[ep 07 #150-#176]`. Device's Guuk spot
is one continuous line that reflows into every scene `[ep 09 #81-#111]`. Oddfellows'
Google Password Manager carries one dots motif through every situation, rendered as
textured 3D spheres `[ep 04 S103-S111]`. Julian Frost's walk cycles put one silhouette
figure into a wireframe cube from every angle `[ep 04 #494-#531]`. Antonio Vicentini
puts cream paint marks over archival stills for four minutes `[ep 04 #370-#410]`.

### Type is quiet, contextual, and treated as an object

The strongest type piece in the playlist is Oddfellows' On Type with Erik Spiekermann,
and its argument is that type belongs in its habitat rather than on top of a frame.
Type appears in a font-picker dropdown scrolling real face names `[ep 05 #78-#81]`, on
an LED dot-matrix panel `[ep 03 cells #132-#133]`, on a newspaper, a Biscuit box and a
Milk carton `[ep 05 #91-#96]`, and in a live text field with a blinking cursor typing
`COMF|` `[ep 03 cell #150]`. "seeing something that is so familiar to designers
beautifully elevated in animation is a real treat" `[ep 03 S111-S119]`.

Where type is not in a UI it is physical. "HOLDAT" is 3D block letters that crack the
court and get hosed down `[ep 07 #242, #248]`. "BRAND" is pressed into wet mortar
`[ep 07 #255]`. Anifilm's letters stand on pedestals and ride in cars `[ep 09 #403-#427]`.
Babel's title is extruded stone with the same speckle as the architecture `[ep 06 #48]`.
"Story Time" is made of clouds `[ep 07 #329]`.

Where type is a layer it is either a caption (Dinosaurs in Love sets one hand-lettered
line per gag in the lower third, `[ep 08 #51-#62]`), a static tagline (Mutual of Omaha
puts white caps top right and leaves them, `[ep 04 #322-#347]`), or a card. The 2019
record puts it well: "Type is either enormous and tracked out, or absent. There is no
mid-weight, mid-size body type anywhere in the showcased work" `[ep 13]`.

Man vs Machine's Spotify piece is the only genuinely maximalist type work in the
playlist and it stays disciplined: the photography never bleeds outside its rectangle,
and the headline is tiled as both the background and the foreground but never the
middle `[ep 10 #122-#137]`.

### Sound is named rarely, and always as an elevation

Six mentions in fourteen episodes: "the music and sound design really elevate it to the
next level" (Crack, `[ep 10 #153-#157]`); "harmonizing what's happening on screen with
the music track" (Austin Bauwens, `[ep 02 S356-S357]`); "very modern motion design even
down to the sound" (Sky rebrand, `[ep 12 S316-S320]`); "amazing sound" (Rocket Panda,
`[ep 10 #517-#525]`); "a really clever use of looping elements that perfectly suits the
music" (Ben Hill, `[ep 04 S129-S135]`); and the Best Breakdown criterion, "they really
got you engaged with the music and the pacing" (Dylan Casano, `[ep 08 #280-#283]`).

That is a low rate for a channel about motion design. It does not mean sound does not
matter. It means that at showcase level the visible differentiators are palette,
transition, scale and concept, and sound is only remarked on when it is unusually
good or unusually integrated.

### Duration is a design decision and short is a compliment

Stated three separate times in one episode: "it's just 22 seconds long and it has
brilliant posing framing perspective and just top-tier action animation" (POTTO,
`[ep 06 #124-#129]`); "best of all it is short this is just a few seconds long i think
this is pretty much the perfect reel intro" (Otilija, `[ep 06 #470-#476]`); "they're
definitely pushing the limits of how much animation you can squeeze into your eyeballs
in as few seconds as possible" (Between Two & Three, `[ep 06 #426-#430]`).

The extreme case is a 12-frame loop: "it might be only 10 or 12 frames by the way he
slices the onion and it just folds into the already sliced section the tears run down
it's an awesome economic use of frames where 12 frames can easily make me watch for 20
or 30 seconds" `[ep 14 T-410-T-414]`. And a two-second one: "probably my favorite two
second loop of the whole year this wavy transformation is just so fun" (Otilija Mo's
letter R, `[ep 14 T-373-T-377]`).

The inverse test is subjective duration: Giant Ant's Twitch spot, "the 60 seconds of
these animations feels like they go by in six" `[ep 03 S86-S89]`.

### Easing character is named only on loops and character work

Four mentions across roughly 200 pieces:

- "the easing on the bounce of this ball feels completely natural but still has
  personality" (aescripts NFT, `[ep 02 S230-S232]`)
- "so many little details with anticipation and overshoot and everything's in there"
  (cassette loop, `[ep 09 Screens 513-516]`)
- "the way the characters spin and the easing on their bounce you could have easily
  animated this way more simpler" (Not Real babushka, `[ep 10 #565-#579]`)
- "there's so much good anticipation in this and little details and secondary motion in
  the fabric and the shoes and of course the great smears on the spin" (Pablo Rojas,
  `[ep 10 #503-#512]`)

All four are short character or object loops. Not one of the long-form award winners
is praised for its eases. That is the most useful calibration in this document: easing
is invisible when it is right, and the things that make a piece read as professional
in a showcase are upstream of it. Build the curves correctly and then spend the
remaining effort on palette, transition and scale.

Two techniques the canon would expect to see and that are effectively absent:

- **Arcs on graphic elements.** Almost nothing swoops. Type, cards and panels travel
  straight or are masked. The one arc family that appears is character work (limbs,
  thrown objects, the ostrich neck) and orbital motifs (Apeel's rings, avatar clouds).
- **Overshoot on anything that is not a toy or a character.** Named four times, all on
  short loops. Never on a title card, a stat, a logo or a camera.

## Style families

Ten families that recur, with what an HTML plus GSAP author can and cannot reach in
each. The "cannot" column matters as much as the "can": several families are entirely
asset-bound, and the honest move is to borrow their design system rather than pretend
to generate their surfaces.

### 1. Flat-vector locked palette

Exemplars: The Lonely Orbit `[ep 03, 06]`, Natalie Labarre's Away `[ep 10]`, Eido's
Rosa Parks `[ep 08, 14]`, Vanitas `[ep 10]`, Anifilm 2020 `[ep 09]`, Doug Alberts'
Enneagram `[ep 08, 11]`, Nataly Menjivar's Parts of a Plant `[ep 11]`.

Colour habits: two to five flats, no gradients except where a gradient is the subject.
Shadows are single hard shapes offset from the caster, not soft falloff. Grain over
everything at low opacity.

Motion character: hard cuts between scenes; entrances are slides, pops and masked
reveals; almost no arcs; almost no overshoot except on props.

HTML plus GSAP: this is the family DOM was built for. Everything is reachable
including the hard shadows (a duplicated solid shape tweened in lockstep), the grain
(a fixed-seed `feTurbulence` overlay at `multiply`), the palette (CSS custom
properties tweened at scene boundaries). The only gap is per-frame line wobble.

### 2. Swiss reductionist shape grammar

Exemplars: Andrew Vucko's Design In Motion `[ep 06 #377-#406]`, Colophon `[ep 01]`,
Apeel `[ep 07]`, the Enneagram `[ep 11]`, Between Two & Three `[ep 06]`.

Colour habits: one solid ground per shot, one or two shapes, flat primaries or one
pastel kit reused across every shot.

Motion character: one shape doing one motion per shot. "it reduces the design down to
its most basic shapes and animation down to the most fundamental motion and you can
just feel how deliberate and thought out all of these choices were" `[ep 06 #385-#392]`.
Cuts are hard and the shot count is high.

HTML plus GSAP: fully reproducible, and the cheapest professional-looking family to
build. The chevron shutter wipe at `[ep 06 #390-#393]` is a row of coloured divs
tweened on `x` behind an `overflow: hidden` parent.

### 3. Grainy painterly cel

Exemplars: Giant Ant's Right Now `[ep 08, 11]`, Hue and Cry's Into the Flame
`[ep 13]`, Nexus Studios' Tell Your Story `[ep 07]`, Dylan Glynn's Wonder Makes Me
Soar `[ep 05]`, Minima Manifesto `[ep 02]`, Illustration for Motion `[ep 13]`.

Colour habits: one warm range per shot, everything not lit crushed to silhouette,
visible brush and paper texture over flat fills.

Motion character: slow parallax between painted layers rather than 3D camera moves;
frame-by-frame character acting; boil on every edge.

HTML plus GSAP: the design system transfers (letterbox, one-source lighting, silhouette,
mirrored reflections, stipple, per-shot palette). The acting does not. Build from
supplied artwork, animate the layers and the light, and be honest that you borrowed the
system, not the animation.

### 4. One-bit, dither and print process

Exemplars: Joel Plosz's The Number Thirteen `[ep 03, 07]`, Reece Parker's TimeLord
`[ep 09]`, Danaé Gosset's Low Island `[ep 09]`, Zack Lydon's Crushed `[ep 01]`,
Jason Yuan and Kelly Anderson `[ep 12]`.

Colour habits: two values, or two or three inks with visible misregistration. Every
midtone is a dot, hatch or dither pattern.

Motion character: hard-edged shadows because the medium forbids soft ones. Loops used
as economy: "really effective and economic use of loops and effects to keep it
interesting for that length of time" `[ep 07 #190-#196]`.

HTML plus GSAP: a static dither is straightforward (a tiled SVG pattern through
`-webkit-mask-image`). The difficulty is that real one-bit work locks the dither cells
to the screen rather than to the moving object; a `position: fixed` pattern layer
masked by the moving shape gets close `[ep 03]`. Physical print registration is out.

### 5. Gradient field and airbrush

Exemplars: Mathijs Luijten's Night `[ep 08]`, Can Drugs Take The Place of Religion?
`[ep 08, 12]`, Buck's Illumina `[ep 01]`, Otilija Morozaite `[ep 06]`, Allen Laseter's
Reshaping Design `[ep 11]`, Johan Eriksson's Crack `[ep 10]`.

Colour habits: figure and ground share one colour ramp so there is no hard boundary
between them; value contrast deliberately low.

Motion character: the fill is animated, not only the position. "even the gradients in
the sphere that's the focal point are animated. It's a small detail but things like
that can really go a long way to set an animation apart" `[ep 02 S106-S112]`.

HTML plus GSAP: strong fit. Register CSS custom properties with `@property` so the
browser interpolates real colour, stack two or three gradient layers at different blend
modes, and blur one for the airbrush softness `[ep 08]`. This is one of the few families
where the DOM version can be genuinely close.

### 6. Kinetic type in context

Exemplars: Oddfellows' On Type and On Color `[ep 03, 05]`, Man vs Machine's Spotify
`[ep 10]`, Reshaping Design `[ep 11]`, aescripts NFT `[ep 02]`.

Colour habits: editorial. Primaries on cream, or one saturated ground per band.

Motion character: type is set, cropped, restacked, struck through and revised on
screen. Words scatter and resolve into a sentence; an acronym expands in place;
a word is struck and replaced above it `[ep 11 #317-#328]`.

HTML plus GSAP: this is HTML's native material and the family with the highest
faithful-reproduction ceiling in the whole playlist. Real DOM text, a real
`@font-face` family with all its weights, `SplitText`-style spans, `clip-path` masks,
a `steps()` caret. The one hard constraint is that every build-time measurement must be
gated on `await document.fonts.ready` or it bakes in fallback metrics.

### 7. Toy plastic and soft 3D product

Exemplars: Bullpen's Fun and Games `[ep 08, 12]`, Frame's Panthella `[ep 06]`,
Between Two & Three `[ep 06]`, BRO&SIS's Sky `[ep 12]`, Ditroit's Pantene `[ep 11]`,
Tendril's Microsoft Icons `[ep 09]`, Oddfellows' Google Password Manager `[ep 04]`.

Colour habits: flat saturated background that changes per beat, one object family, real
macro depth of field.

Motion character: minimal and mechanical. A slider slides, a wheel turns, the camera
dollies a few centimetres. Wide shot, then push in with shallow focus, then cut.

HTML plus GSAP: the structure transfers exactly (flat ground swap per beat, wide-to-macro
cut rhythm, staged `filter: blur()` per depth layer for a rack focus). The materials do
not. Subsurface, fillet highlights and reflections that change with camera position are
render-time. A CSS gradient standing in for that falloff "reads as a sticker" `[ep 06]`.

### 8. Three-D imitating stop motion

Exemplars: BUCK's Mailchimp shorts `[ep 03, 07]`, ROOF Studio's Rosemarie's Life
`[ep 05]`, The Rusted Pixel's Jack's Patch `[ep 10]`, Harry Bhalerao `[ep 13]`.

Colour habits: warm, real-material, tabletop lighting with short falloff.

Motion character: the read comes from two things and the narrator names both. Materials
at the right scale ("all the cloth is super realistic and looks like it's made out of
scaled-up cloth", `[ep 05 #337-#347]`) and mixed frame rates inside one shot.

HTML plus GSAP: the timing half is fully reproducible and is the more transferable half.
Drive the timeline through a ticker that quantises time per segment to 1, 2, 4, 8 or
16 fps `[ep 07]`. The materials are not reachable.

### 9. Mixed-media collage

Exemplars: Ariel Costa's History of Horsepower `[ep 03, 07]`, Bito's Get Wild Award
`[ep 09]`, Chico Jofilsan's In My Particular Case `[ep 08, 12]`, Dima Maseykin's Keith
Haring `[ep 11]`, Elastic's Google Trillions `[ep 09]`, Sara Serna's DMT `[ep 11]`.

Colour habits: whatever the sources are, unified by one grain and one vignette over the
whole frame. That overlay is the only thing holding it together `[ep 03]`.

Motion character: simple position and rotation tweens on cut-out layers, and a deliberate
escape from the puppet-pin default: "normally super cheesy ... but here they lean into
that and they get so detailed ... they made a lot of new stuff and put care into every
shot even though they may only last a few frames" `[ep 09 Screens 361-402]`.

HTML plus GSAP: the motion is easy once the assets exist. The value is in the asset
library. Budget accordingly.

### 10. The frame as a character

Exemplars: Jonathan Djob Nkondo's The New Exhibition `[ep 03, 07]`, Ordinary Folk's
Gospel `[ep 03, 06]`, Yuval Hacker's Covid Loop `[ep 12]`, Joel Plosz's fixed
proscenium `[ep 07]`, Flatwhite's letterbox and corner play buttons `[ep 03]`,
Nexus's rectangle pushed by hands `[ep 07]`, VW's practical viewfinder `[ep 07]`.

Colour habits: usually severe, because the device needs the eye on geometry.

Motion character: the picture boundary is animated. Nkondo folds two wall planes so
that each cut is a re-angling of the plane rather than a camera move; characters hide
behind plane edges, poke out of them and are cropped by them `[ep 07 #303-#319]`.

HTML plus GSAP: unusually well suited, because the source is already flat planes in a
void. A `perspective` container with two child faces joined at a shared edge, animated
on `rotateY`, each face carrying its own content. A three-value monochrome palette makes
the anti-aliasing seams invisible `[ep 03]`.

## The narrator's rules, consolidated

Deduplicated across all fourteen episodes and ordered by how many episodes state a
version of the rule.

1. **Transitions are where the effort shows.** Twelve episodes. "they could have just
   gone for a very easy cut ... but the effort to make them flow together nicely into
   one another really sets us apart" `[ep 04 S152-S162]`. Corollaries: the transition
   object belongs to both scenes; a match cut on shared motion is "relatively easy to
   make" and "can add a clever layer of sophistication" `[ep 02 S401-S406]`; carry
   motion direction through a cut `[ep 02 S313-S317]`; transitions should carry story
   information, not just conceal a join `[ep 05 #348-#355]`.

2. **Restrict the palette so the viewer can see the forms.** Nine episodes. "each
   scene only has three colors so you can concentrate on the forms" `[ep 01 #36-#37]`.
   Corollary: restriction first, then variation inside the restriction `[ep 06 #350]`.
   Corollary: map colour to story themes rather than to decoration `[ep 02 S264-S267]`.

3. **Texture makes computed motion feel handmade; apply it late and selectively.**
   Eight episodes. Nerdo's ladder (rough, solid fills, selective edge blur on lit edges
   only, grain on the blur, colour and detail) `[ep 01 #40-#43]` is the reference
   process. Corollary: texture must be consistent across character and environment
   `[ep 07 #330-#331]`. Corollary: doing it physically beats faking it, and you can
   tell `[ep 09 Screens 287-293]`, `[ep 10 #100-#106]`.

4. **Style-hopping works when one device holds it together.** Six episodes. "multiple
   different styles 2D 3D and live action all mashed into one ... manages to hold
   together through a central theme of a duck" `[ep 01 #5-#6]`. The device can be a
   border `[ep 06]`, a hand-off object `[ep 07]`, a shelf grid `[ep 08]`, a running
   gag `[ep 10]`, or one shared base animation `[ep 02]`.

5. **Blend 2D and 3D so the seam is invisible; each medium needs a job.** Six
   episodes. "smart use of 3d elements which i didn't even notice that they were 3d
   until i saw those in the credits" `[ep 06 #113-#117]`. The mechanism named is shared
   lighting: "the way they blend together especially the colors and the lighting and how
   they interact between the background and the characters" `[ep 04 S38-S48]`. He judges
   3D-made-to-look-2D strictly: "i'm particularly nitpicky when it comes to 3d assets
   made to look like 2d but this is one of the best i've seen" `[ep 14 T-244-T-247]`.

6. **Design carries the piece; the animation is allowed to be small.** Six episodes.
   "design is the most important thing in all of animation" `[ep 13 S80-S87]`; "great
   animation isn't necessarily more animation" `[ep 11 #68-#69]`; "the animation is
   really subtle just because the design and the concept is so well" `[ep 08 #126-#130]`.

7. **Scale and composition are storytelling tools.** Five episodes. "the character is
   just tiny in her environment and that really helps the story and really guide your eye
   to be looking at what's important" `[ep 09 Screens 219-234]`; "you get this massive
   contrast between something being so small and something being so massive"
   `[ep 06 #355-#365]`.

8. **Publish your process; roughs are content.** Five episodes. "i love a good breakdown
   i love seeing the roughs the cleaned up animation and then the final thing"
   `[ep 10 #482-#486]`; "a breakdown is a piece of content, not a slide deck"
   `[ep 08 #280-#283]`; show the problem, then the solution `[ep 08 #306-#312]`; "this is
   the unused animation ... you know what made it has got to be spectacular"
   `[ep 14 T-404-T-409]`.

9. **Short is a compliment; density per second is the metric.** Five episodes. Twenty-two
   seconds, two seconds and twelve frames are all praised on those grounds
   `[ep 06 #124-#129]`, `[ep 14 T-373-T-377]`, `[ep 14 T-410-T-414]`. The subjective test:
   "the 60 seconds of these animations feels like they go by in six" `[ep 03 S86-S89]`.

10. **Smears sell speed and are authored, not derived.** Five episodes. "i love smears,
    it really helps sell the illusion of speed as the circles whip through their motion
    paths" `[ep 02 S234-S237]`; clay smears "mimic the motion blur in clay"
    `[ep 11 #164-#171]`; Numecaniq's breakdown shows the smears drawn as their own
    shapes with the character removed `[ep 08 #285-#286]`.

11. **The frame boundary is a narrative device.** Four episodes, and stated twice in a
    row inside one of them, which the 2021 record calls "the strongest signal in the
    episode" `[ep 03]`. "they used the frame and the composition to really tell the great
    story itself" `[ep 03 S255-S259]`; "the characters are interacting with the frame to
    switch the camera angles and build tension" `[ep 03 S278-S286]`.

12. **The character-animation checklist: anticipation, overshoot, secondary motion,
    smears.** Four episodes, and it is the only place easing is discussed at all.
    "so many little details with anticipation and overshoot and everything's in there"
    `[ep 09 Screens 513-516]`; "there's so much good anticipation in this and little
    details and secondary motion in the fabric and the shoes and of course the great
    smears on the spin" `[ep 10 #503-#512]`.

13. **One motif carried through every situation.** Four episodes. "the way they use their
    dots motif to show a hidden password and represent them as textured spheres
    throughout this piece" `[ep 04 S103-S109]`.

14. **Light does the composition.** Four episodes. "the main character is basically only
    visible by the rim light and the whole frame is being segmented by these stark fields
    of light and dark" `[ep 02 S255-S258]`; "the use of light which is obviously
    thematically relevant but works so well in these designs too" `[ep 06 #157-#163]`.

15. **Personal projects contain the most of their maker.** Four episodes. "this is where
    the artists get to put as much of themselves into the project" `[ep 08 #326-#335]`;
    "embrace what makes your work different and put as much of that in as possible"
    `[ep 10 #46-#52]`; short loops deserve the same attention as work that "cost hundreds
    of thousands of dollars to produce" `[ep 09 Screens 483-484]`.

16. **Split the object into parts and stagger them.** Four episodes. "a run cycle in the
    middle where the torso just starts rotating around itself while the limbs keep
    moving" `[ep 14 T-155-T-160]`; "i love the delayed reaction to the eyes where one eye
    animates before the other one" `[ep 14 T-398-T-400]`; "i just love how its neck enters
    the frame and winds its way into this position" `[ep 14 T-364-T-365]`.

17. **Hold with confidence; pacing needs quiet as well as action.** Three episodes.
    "it's just so confident in the way that it holds on these shots for a good amount of
    time and lets you take in what's on screen" `[ep 09 Screens 201-211]`; "the pacing is
    done so well there are those quiet moments but there are huge moments of action"
    `[ep 09 Screens 460-463]`.

18. **Mixed and reduced frame rates are a choice, and can vary inside one shot.** Three
    episodes. The 1/2/4/8/16 fps note `[ep 07 #131-#147]`; the objects-on-a-lower-rate-
    than-the-camera note `[ep 12 S57-S72]`; "i love how he uses the low frame rate"
    `[ep 07 #234]`.

19. **Sound is part of the system, not a garnish.** Three episodes. "the music and sound
    design really elevate it to the next level" `[ep 10 #153-#157]`; "very modern motion
    design even down to the sound" `[ep 12 S316-S320]`.

20. **Warp the perspective; it is animation, not a lens.** Two episodes. "i always revert
    to just the most basic layout in perspective when there's really no need it's
    animation you can do anything" `[ep 10 #180-#188]`; "we don't need to live in the real
    world we can warp the perspective if we want to get a better angle on a cork on the
    edge of a bottle" `[ep 06 #490-#499]`.

21. **Do not let the client category dictate the style.** Two episodes. "it is certainly
    not the first sort of style that comes to mind when you think of an animation for
    bmw" `[ep 07 #70-#75]`.

22. **Level of detail should track camera distance.** One episode, but stated as a
    principle. "i love how when the scenes zoom out the details just get more simplified"
    `[ep 06 #313-#317]`.

23. **End on a settle.** One episode. "i love this last shot where all these cubes sort of
    settle down and you see the full suite of the products in a kitchen"
    `[ep 09 Screens 347-353]`.

24. **Deliberateness is the top-line criterion.** Stated twice in one episode about two
    different pieces. "every inch and every decision looks so deliberate"
    `[ep 06 #54]`; "you can just feel how deliberate and thought out all of these choices
    were" `[ep 06 #396]`.

25. **Meta-rule: this is taste, not authority.** Four episodes. "i am only one man with
    two eyes so i cannot see everything" `[ep 08 #11-#24]`; "obviously saying what's the
    best animation is completely subjective" `[ep 08 #192-#194]`; "i've got maybe a bias
    towards more 2d illustrative animation" `[ep 14 T-425-T-428]`; and the useful version,
    "you under-value craft in media you do not practise" `[ep 08 #198-#216]`.

## Where this confirms, extends, or contradicts the canon

### Confirms

**`twelve-principles.md` §3 Staging and `motion-rules.md` Rule 9 (the stagger cap and
the one-third rule).** Directly confirmed by the pieces the narrator singles out for
economy. Jonas Mosesson's onion loop moves the knife, one slice and two tears; the chef
is static `[ep 14 #496-#511]`. Strange Beast holds a whole diner and lifts only the
noodles `[ep 14 #364-#367]`. CRCR holds the camera and lets one ant do all the work
`[ep 14 #108-#111]`. Bullpen wins Best Design 2020 on a slider `[ep 08]`.

**`motion-rules.md` Rule 10 (hold and breath).** "it's just so confident in the way that
it holds on these shots for a good amount of time and lets you take in what's on screen"
(Joyce Ho, Resonance, `[ep 09 Screens 201-211]`) is the same claim the rule makes from
the other direction. The record for episode 03 adds the comic form: "Holds carry the
comedy. In every stop-motion entry the funny beat is a held pose, not a move."

**`motion-rules.md` Rule 11 (rhythm contrast).** "the pacing is done so well there are
those quiet moments but there are huge moments of action" `[ep 09 Screens 460-463]`.

**`twelve-principles.md` §5 (follow-through, overlapping action, and the moving hold).**
Four independent confirmations, all praised as the specific reason a piece works: the
frog's second eye `[ep 14]`, the crocodile's leaf undersides folding for "only a few
frames" `[ep 09 Screens 528-530]`, the hat flying off during Anifilm's 3D spin
`[ep 09 #403-#409]`, and Florian Perron's cloth folds `[ep 14 T-386-T-392]`.

**`twelve-principles.md` §1 (squash and stretch, with volume preserved).** Golden Wolf's
unused wedding-cake tie-down shows a heavy squash with icing splashing off the sides at
the moment of contact and the top tier separating on the rebound `[ep 14 #491-#493]`.
Tyler Morgan's corn pigs "do these wonderful bounces" `[ep 09 Screens 520-524]`.

**`twelve-principles.md` §14.1 (the masked reveal as the default gesture).** The playlist
supplies four independent instances of a mask that stays fixed while content moves
through it or grows to fill: Ordinary Folk's rectangular mattes `[ep 06 #164-#172]`,
ILLO's card-in-card wipes `[ep 06 #283-#296]`, Man vs Machine's photo window
`[ep 10 #122-#137]`, Ryan Bird's receding tunnel `[ep 10 #533-#535]`.

**`twelve-principles.md` §14.3 (luminance is a motion channel).** Confirmed four times,
and always as a cut rather than as an accent: the Tycho lamp blooms until it whites out
the frame `[ep 14 #72-#74]`, Ordinary Folk's Gospel spends its whole running time at low
key so the terminal white-out lands as an event `[ep 03 cells #344-#346]`, CRCR reveals
a fly inside a radial light burst `[ep 14 #121]`, and Ordinary Folk's reel collapses to
a single white dot on black before its colour section `[ep 13 F66]`.

**`grading-rubric.md` C16 (palette discipline) and C17 (transition design).** Both
confirmed harder than the rubric states them. Palette is the number-one observed
property and transition craft the number two; the rubric weights them as two criteria
among eighteen.

### Extends

**Mixed frame rates inside a single shot.** Nothing in the canon composes two cadences
in one shot. `twelve-principles.md` M9 concedes a declared cadence ("`posterize: 2`
makes every 2-frame duplicate correct") but assumes one cadence per composition. BUCK
runs 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 fps in one shot `[ep 07 #131-#147]`; Amigo Total runs the
objects on a low rate under a camera on a high one `[ep 12 S57-S72]`. This is a
genuinely new parameter: cadence per layer, per segment.

**Level of detail as a function of camera distance.** `viewport-change` warns that
scaling the world down "drops every label below legible pixel size" but prescribes no
remedy beyond authoring at 1x. 3:45 PM's answer is to swap the art: faces, then limbs,
then colour blobs, then a mosaic `[ep 06 #306-#321]`. The Lonely Orbit does the same to
a star-chart dome, which loses its network lines and becomes a dot field as the shot
widens `[ep 06 #356-#364]`.

**Texture as a spatially targeted compositional tool.** `twelve-principles.md` §14.4
treats grain as a delivery aid: "1-2% grain or dither, which also gives the encoder
something to hold onto". Nerdo's use is the opposite: grain applied only to the blurred
edges that catch the light, hard edges left clean, so the texture reads as lighting
`[ep 01 #38-#43]`. That is a different rule with a different measurable signature.

**The frame edge as an animated character.** The canon has masked reveals and a fixed
safe margin. It has nothing for a picture plane that folds to change the camera angle
`[ep 03 cells #353-#358]`, a decorative border that redraws per section
`[ep 06 #77-#99]`, or a matte that carries the incoming scene and grows to fill
`[ep 06 #164-#172]`.

**Single-take structure with speed as the pacing device.** `motion-rules.md` Rule 8 and
Rule 11 both put pacing contrast across cuts, with blank runs of 1 to 8 frames doing the
breathing. Coat of Arms' Cloud puts the entire contrast inside one uncut camera move
that "speeds up during transitions and slows down to show each scene", and the stated
benefit is that "it's hard to look away because there's no actual breaks"
`[ep 02 S62-S72]`. That is a whole structural alternative the canon does not name.

**Anthology coherence.** Rule 8's object persistence covers one match cut. The playlist
needs a rule for N unrelated visual systems in one piece held by one persistent element,
which happens at least twelve times `[ep 06, 07, 08, 09, 10]`.

**The rough pass as a deliverable.** `twelve-principles.md` §14.4 says "block first,
curve later" and warns that a grader keyed to the finished register will reject a
blocking pass. The playlist goes further: the rough is publishable and is judged on its
own. Golden Wolf's unused tie-down is in an episode on its merits `[ep 14 #475-#495]`,
and the whole Best Breakdown category is an argument that a process piece should be
paced and scored like a film `[ep 08]`.

**Refraction and dispersion as a signature finish.** Not in the canon at all, and it
appears in ten pieces `[ep 02, 08, 11, 12, 13]`. Chromatic aberration on 2D artwork is
specifically defended as a language choice: "they are using the language of cinema,
light captured through a lens and through a sensor in a way that we don't really see
with our eyes ... it gives us the feeling that it's cinematic" `[ep 02 S36-S56]`.

### Contradicts

**`motion-rules.md` Rule 17 and `grade-original.py`'s zero-duplicate-frames check.**
A 30 fps render of BUCK's 1 fps section is thirty identical frames, and the whole
technique is praised specifically for that `[ep 07 #131-#147]`. The same applies to
every stop-motion mimicry piece in the playlist and to Giant Ant's single-beat pose
cards `[ep 03 cells #93, #95, #105]`. `twelve-principles.md` M9's "declared cadence"
exemption is the right shape but is written for one global cadence, not for a per-layer,
per-segment declaration.

**`twelve-principles.md` §5(e) and `grade-original.py`'s "settle direction reversals = 0"
law.** Contradicted by the loops the narrator praises for exactly that reversal: "so
many little details with anticipation and overshoot and everything's in there"
`[ep 09 Screens 513-516]`, "the easing on their bounce" `[ep 10 #565-#579]`. The canon
already carries the escape hatch (overshoot is a register), but the register table has no
row for "short character or object loop", which is where all four of the playlist's
easing compliments live.

**`motion-rules.md` Rule 14's film-versus-UI duration table.** The rule flags "a
composition whose 90th percentile tween duration is under 0.5 s and whose longest hold
is under 1.5 s" as probably built on UI numbers. The playlist's two most-praised short
pieces are a two-second letter-R loop `[ep 14 T-373-T-377]` and a twelve-frame onion
loop `[ep 14 T-410-T-414]`. Neither has a 1.5 s hold and neither is built on UI numbers;
they are a third register, the loop, and the canon has no row for it. A 15-second piece
that contains a loop should not be graded against the held-card table.

**`motion-rules.md` Rule 1 ("nothing starts on the same frame").** Contradicted by two
whole projects whose concept is the opposite. The Bounce collab gives twenty-plus
animators one identical base bounce so the film becomes "a series of smooth match cuts"
`[ep 02 S401-S406]`; Infinite Journeys gives every artist the same base camera move
`[ep 02 S382-S393]`. Deliberate simultaneity across a group is a technique, not a defect.
`twelve-principles.md` §3(d) already carves out "same-frame starts that land on a declared
audio hit"; this needs the same carve-out for a declared shared motion.

**`twelve-principles.md` §7(e) on arcs, in its revised form, is confirmed rather than
contradicted, and the older LottieFiles position it corrects is contradicted.** The
revised text says type, cards, panels and UI travel straight and that an arc on a text
element should be reported as unmotivated. Across 14 episodes and roughly 200 pieces
there is essentially no swooping graphic motion. Arcs appear on characters, thrown
objects and orbital motifs only.

**The canon's easing-first framing, as a matter of emphasis.** `motion-rules.md` Rule 4
puts the graph editor at the centre of professional practice and cites Ben Marriott's own
interview to do it: "my work plateaued ... nothing flowed smoothly", then "a plugin diet"
and "everything inside the graph editor". That is his account of learning to animate.
Across fourteen episodes of him judging other people's finished work, easing is named
four times and never on a long-form winner. Both are true. The reconciliation: correct
easing is a threshold you cross once and then stop being able to see, and what separates
finished professional work from other finished professional work is upstream of it. A
grader should keep the ease checks as gates and stop treating them as the score.

**`sound-design.md`'s "a film whose cuts are silent reads unfinished".** Not contradicted
as a rule, but calibrated: six mentions in fourteen episodes. Sound is a floor, not a
differentiator, at this level.

## Recipes worth adding to the skill

Fifteen, ordered roughly by how often the underlying technique appears in the playlist.
Each is tagged NEW (nothing in `rules-index.md` covers it) or EXTENDS (an existing rule
covers the mechanism but not this use).

### 1. `stepped-cadence-mix` (NEW)

**What it is.** Two or more cadences running in one shot: one layer stepped to 2, 4, 8
or 12 fps while another runs at the render rate.

**Exemplar.** BUCK, Mailchimp "All in a Day's Work", `[ep 07 #113-#149]`, narration at
`#131-#147`: "a mixture of 1 2 4 8 and even 16 frames per second in a single shot".
Second exemplar: Amigo Total, Practices for XR Design, `[ep 12 #63-#94]`, objects on a
low rate under a camera on a high one.

**Mechanism.** Do not step the timeline. Step the value. For each stepped layer, wrap
its driver in a quantiser: a proxy tween with `ease: "none"` whose `onUpdate` computes
`q = Math.floor(t * layerFps) / layerFps` and writes the layer's pose from `q`, so the
layer holds between its own frames while the camera wrapper tweens continuously off the
same timeline time. The cadence is a declared per-layer, per-segment constant, not a
global setting, and it must be recorded in the composition manifest so a grader knows
that duplicate frames on that layer are correct. Never mix a stepped layer and a smooth
layer on the same element; put them on parent and child.

**Measurable signature.** On the layer's centroid step series (M6), the diffs are zero
for `renderFps / layerFps - 1` frames and then jump; the jump magnitudes are
approximately equal. The camera wrapper's step series over the same window is smooth and
non-zero on every frame. Duplicate-frame count for the layer region is
`renderFps / layerFps - 1` per stepped interval and is 0 for the frame overall.

### 2. `persistent-handoff-object` (EXTENDS `card-morph-anchor`, `scale-swap-transition`)

**What it is.** One element holds position, scale and identity while the world behind it
is replaced N times. The cut is invisible because the eye is locked on the survivor.

**Exemplar.** Gabriele Calvi, Vanitas: the red circle sits at or near frame centre in ten
consecutive shots while the field swaps between red, violet and black and the
architecture rebuilds around it `[ep 08 #63-#72]`. Second exemplar: TEDx Sydney REAL
passes a circle from a phone screen to a sphere to a torus portal across ten studios
`[ep 07 #5, #13, #86, #92-#93]`.

**Mechanism.** The survivor is a single DOM node that lives outside every scene wrapper,
rendered above them, with a `tl.set` baseline at t=0 so a render worker seeking past a
scene boundary cannot latch it hidden. Scene swaps are `tl.set` visibility changes on
the wrappers at the cut frame. The survivor gets a slow continuous property of its own
(a scale drift, a hue tween, a rotation) so it is never bit-identical across the cut,
and one property may change identity at a chosen beat (Vanitas inverts red-on-field to
black-on-red at the end, `[ep 08 #73]`), which is what stops the device reading as a
static overlay.

**Measurable signature.** Track the survivor region's centroid across every cut frame:
displacement across the cut is under 1% of frame width and the second-moment ratio
`sx/sy` changes by under 2%. Everything outside the survivor region changes by more than
40% of its ink count on the same frame.

### 3. `matte-card-grow` (EXTENDS `card-morph-anchor`)

**What it is.** A hard-edged rectangle carrying the incoming scene sits over the outgoing
shot and scales up to full bleed. It is a transition, not a container morph: nothing
about the outgoing shot changes.

**Exemplar.** Ordinary Folk, Christianity Explored, `[ep 06 #164-#172]`: a nail against
black over a bright desert, a bare tree in a dark card over the same desert, an apple in
a dark card. The record calls it "the transition device" for the whole piece.

**Mechanism.** An absolutely positioned layer holds the incoming scene at full size, with
`clip-path: inset(...)` tweened from a small centred rectangle to `0%`. Because the
content is authored at full size and only the clip changes, the incoming scene has no
scale artefacts and no leading edge entering the frame. `power3.inOut` on the inset;
the outgoing scene never animates. Optional 1 to 2 px hairline via a border on the clip
wrapper, which is what makes the card read as an object rather than as a wipe.

**Measurable signature.** Per frame, the incoming region is a rectangle whose width and
height both grow monotonically and whose centre is fixed within 1% of frame dimensions.
The outgoing region's step series (M6) is identically zero for the whole transition
window. Duration 0.35 to 0.6 s at the playlist's cutting rate.

### 4. `luminance-cut` (NEW; `ambient-glow-bloom` caps at 0.45 and forbids this trigger)

**What it is.** A full-frame brightness event used as the cut itself, rather than as an
accent. Bloom to white, hold one to three frames, resolve on the new scene.

**Exemplar.** Mathijs Luijten, Weather (Tycho): a hand reaches to a glowing crescent lamp
and the bloom grows over three frames until it whites out the frame `[ep 14 #72-#74]`.
Second exemplar: Ordinary Folk's Gospel spends its running time at very low key so the
terminal white bloom is the payoff `[ep 03 cells #344-#346]`.

**Mechanism.** A full-cover layer, `opacity: 0`, `background: #fff` (or the scene's
lightest value; a pure white on a warm film reads wrong). Tween opacity 0 to 1 on
`power3.in` over 3 to 5 frames, `tl.set` the scene swap at peak, then 0 on `power2.out`
over 5 to 8 frames. The in-ramp is shorter than the out-ramp because the eye adapts
faster to brightening than to darkening. Sell the source: something in the outgoing shot
must be visibly the origin of the light in the two frames before the ramp starts, or the
flash reads as a render error.

**Safety.** `twelve-principles.md` §14.3 is load-bearing here: no more than three
full-frame flashes or luminance reversals in any one-second period (WCAG 2.3.1,
ITU-R BT.1702). A 15-second piece gets at most a handful, and never in a cluster.

**Measurable signature.** Frame mean luma rises monotonically to within 3% of the
lightest available value, holds 1 to 3 frames, then falls. Ink count (M2) goes to
approximately zero at peak. Count flashes per rolling second and assert 3 or fewer.

### 5. `hard-offset-shadow` (NEW)

**What it is.** A cast shadow drawn as a single flat dark copy of the caster, offset,
with no blur and no gradient. The graphic register's answer to depth.

**Exemplar.** Gabriele Calvi, Vanitas: cream bare trees each throwing one hard black
shape `[ep 10 #205]`. Second exemplar: Anna Taberko's UFO loop, "i love when design has
such harsh dark shadows like this" `[ep 11 #446-#447]`. Third: Eric Dias' guillotine,
"i just love the dark shadows on the side of the ground" `[ep 14 T-380-T-381]`.

**Mechanism.** The shadow is a sibling element, not a `box-shadow` and not a
`drop-shadow` filter. It is a duplicate of the caster's silhouette, filled with one
flat value (usually the palette's darkest, not black), offset by a constant vector that
is the same for every object in the film, and skewed if the ground plane is oblique.
It shares the caster's transform tween exactly, because a drop shadow is attached to its
caster; only its opacity and length may lag. On a lift, tween the offset length and the
opacity down together, on a gentler ease than the primary, starting 2 frames late.

**Measurable signature.** The vector from each object's centroid to its shadow's centroid
has the same direction, within a few degrees, for every object in every shot. Shadow
region mean gradient magnitude (M8) is as high as the caster's, which is the test that
separates it from a blurred shadow. Zero drift between caster and shadow centroids
during a translate.

### 6. `locked-palette-contract` (NEW; `theme-crossfade-morph` swaps whole skins, this locks a set)

**What it is.** Declare N colours as tokens at build time, forbid anything outside the
set, and treat any palette change as a scene-level event with narrative meaning.

**Exemplar.** The Lonely Orbit: navy, off-white, and an orange reserved almost entirely
for skin and faces `[ep 06 #337-#376]`. Second: Studio AKA's Bellefaire, one hue per act,
olive then orange then red `[ep 09 #301-#346]`. Third: Giant Ant's Hilda titles, "three
distinct colour schemes to represent the three different themes explored in the feature"
`[ep 02 S264-S267]`.

**Mechanism.** Every colour in the composition is a CSS custom property on `:root`
(`--ink`, `--ground`, `--accent`, `--shade`). No literal colour anywhere in the
stylesheet or in a tween target. Register them with `@property` and `syntax: "<color>"`
so the browser interpolates in colour space rather than as strings. A palette change is
one tween on the token set at a scene boundary, on the same beat as a cut, never
mid-shot. Reserve one token for a single semantic job (skin, the accent, the danger
state) and never use it for anything else; that reservation is what makes a three-colour
film read as designed rather than as limited.

**Measurable signature.** Quantise every rendered frame to its dominant colours and
assert that fewer than N+2 clusters cover 95% of non-antialiased pixels. Token change
frames coincide exactly with cut frames. The reserved accent occupies under 8% of ink in
every frame it appears in.

### 7. `selective-edge-texture` (NEW)

**What it is.** Grain and softening applied only to the edges that face the light, with
the remaining contours left hard. Texture as lighting, not as a finishing overlay.

**Exemplar.** Nerdo, Tencent China Literature, four-stage breakdown at `[ep 01 #40-#43]`.
At #42 the top of the arms, the underside of the slab and the crown of the head are
softened and grainy; the outer arm contours stay hard. Narration at `#38-#39`: "the grain
isn't just a texture that's layered on top of the animation but very controlled and
intentionally used as a way to highlight certain edges and shapes".

**Mechanism.** Duplicate the silhouette. Apply `filter: blur()` plus an SVG
`feTurbulence` and `feDisplacementMap` with a fixed `seed` to the duplicate, then clip it
with a mask that exposes only the lit edges. Because the seed is fixed, the result is
deterministic; to make it boil on twos, quantise the timeline time and step the
`baseFrequency` through a small authored array rather than randomising. The mask is
authored per shot from the film's one implied light direction, which must be the same
direction used by `hard-offset-shadow`.

**Measurable signature.** Along the object's contour, sample the local gradient magnitude
(M8). The lit arc reads 40 to 70% of the unlit arc's value. The high-frequency energy in
the masked band is 3 to 8 times the energy in the flat interior. Both figures are stable
across the shot, so the texture reads as a property of the light rather than as noise.

### 8. `lod-by-scale` (NEW; extends `viewport-change` and `multi-phase-camera`)

**What it is.** Swap the artwork for a simpler version at authored scale thresholds as
the camera pulls back, so detail density stays roughly constant on screen.

**Exemplar.** Alisha Liu, 3:45 PM: two girls on a blanket with facial detail and
individual crisps, then a top-down field of colour blobs where they are two dots, then a
mosaic of blue and green rectangles reading as a city grid `[ep 06 #306-#321]`.
Narration at `#313-#317`: "i love how when the scenes zoom out the details just get more
simplified it is really masterfully done".

**Mechanism.** Author two or three complete versions of the subject at decreasing detail,
stacked at identical geometry. A single scale driver on the world wrapper carries the
camera; a set of `tl.set` calls at authored scale thresholds toggles which version is
visible. Cross the threshold during the fastest part of the camera move, never on a hold,
and never during a settle. Each version keeps the same silhouette and the same palette,
so the swap is invisible; only interior detail is dropped.

**Measurable signature.** Per frame, count edge pixels inside the subject region divided
by the region's area. Without LOD this falls monotonically as the camera pulls back; with
LOD it stays within a band (roughly plus or minus 30% of its opening value). At the swap
frame, the subject's outer contour position changes by under 1 px while its interior edge
count jumps.

### 9. `scale-jump-cut` (NEW; every existing camera rule moves the camera)

**What it is.** A hard cut between two framings of the same world at radically different
scale, with no camera move on either side. The size change is the transition.

**Exemplar.** The Lonely Orbit: a full control room, then a single coffee mug, then a
helmet in extreme close-up, in three cuts `[ep 03 cells #213, #217, #224]`. Second:
Bones, giant skull then extreme face close-up `[ep 09 #153-#167]`. Third: Flatwhite's ox
cropped to eyes only inside a drawn letterbox strip `[ep 03 cell #194]`.

**Mechanism.** Three sibling scene wrappers in one timeline, each with its own
`--scene-scale`, hard cut with `tl.set` at the boundary, no transition of any kind. The
constraint that makes it read as scale rather than as a jump to somewhere new: identical
palette tokens, identical stroke weight relative to the frame, and one recurring object
present in at least two of the three framings. Each framing gets its own single ambient
motion so the cut lands between two live frames, never between two stalls.

**Measurable signature.** Across the cut, the palette cluster set is unchanged, and the
apparent size of the shared object changes by a factor of 4 or more. Both sides of the
cut have non-zero ink-count derivative on the cut frame, so nothing is frozen when the
cut happens.

### 10. `folding-picture-plane` (NEW; `split-tilt-cards` tilts two cards, this folds one surface)

**What it is.** The picture plane is a physical object in a void. It folds at a hinge, and
each cut is a re-angling of the plane rather than a camera move. Characters hide behind
plane edges, poke out of them and are cropped by them.

**Exemplar.** Jonathan Djob Nkondo, The New Exhibition `[ep 03 cells #347-#367]`,
`[ep 07 #302-#319]`. Narration: "the characters are interacting with the frame to switch
the camera angles and build tension in a way that just really progresses the story"
`[ep 03 S278-S286]`.

**Mechanism.** A `perspective` container with two child faces joined at a shared vertical
edge, `transform-style: preserve-3d`, each face carrying its own content and its own
`overflow: hidden`. The hinge angle is one tween on `rotateY` of the second face; a
"cut" is a step in that angle rather than a scene swap. Characters are SVG groups placed
inside a face so the face's overflow crops them, which is what produces the
hide-and-poke-out behaviour for free. Keep the palette to three values so the
anti-aliasing seam at the hinge is invisible. Do not put a `filter` on a child of the
`preserve-3d` group; it flattens the 3D context.

**Measurable signature.** The hinge line is a straight edge whose screen position moves
smoothly while the two faces' apparent widths change in opposite directions with a
constant sum plus perspective foreshortening. Content crossing the hinge disappears at
the seam rather than overlapping it.

### 11. `offset-twin-delay` (NEW; `waterfall-entry` staggers a group, this splits a pair)

**What it is.** Two elements that would naturally move together are offset by 2 to 5
frames, or one part of a rigid body is given its own independent rotation. The
asymmetry is the whole gag.

**Exemplar.** design_blimp's frog: "i love the delayed reaction to the eyes where one eye
animates before the other one it's just great" `[ep 14 T-397-T-400]`. Second: Mehdi
Alibeygi's No.7, "a run cycle in the middle where the torso just starts rotating around
itself while the limbs keep moving" `[ep 14 T-155-T-160]`. Third: Murena Murena's
ostrich, "i just love how its neck enters the frame and winds its way into this position"
before the body arrives `[ep 14 T-364-T-365]`.

**Mechanism.** Identify the pair or part that the viewer expects to be locked. Give the
follower its own `fromTo` with the same ease family and the same duration, offset by
`f(2)` to `f(5)` on the frame grid. For the rigid-part case, put the independent rotation
on a child wrapper so it composes with the parent's travel rather than fighting it. The
offset must be large enough to read at 30 fps (2 frames is the floor) and small enough
that the pair still reads as one object (5 frames is the ceiling before they separate).

**Measurable signature.** Cross-correlate the two regions' step series (M6): the peak
sits at a positive lag of 2 to 5 frames and the correlation at that lag is above 0.8. At
lag 0 the correlation is materially lower. Both regions' travel amplitudes are within 20%
of each other, which is what distinguishes this from follow-through, where the follower's
amplitude is 30 to 50% of the leader's.

### 12. `authored-smear-pose` (EXTENDS `motion-blur-streak`)

**What it is.** A smear is a drawn shape shown for one or two frames, not a blur derived
from velocity. `motion-blur-streak` produces a Gaussian smear or a ghost trail; this
produces a specific silhouette.

**Exemplar.** Rodrigo Miguel (Numecaniq), Rick and Morty Dreams breakdown
`[ep 08 #285-#286]`: a two-panel graphic with a 2x2 grid of key drawings on the left and
the isolated smear shapes on the right, drawn as clean tapered ribbons on white with the
character removed entirely. The episode record's reading is the load-bearing one: "the
smears were authored as their own shapes rather than derived by blur, and they read as
abstract forms on their own". Second exemplar: Mehdi Alibeygi's No.7, where at
`[ep 08 cell #285]` the leg becomes two long parallel strokes with an ellipse head.

**Mechanism.** Three states, not a tween: extreme A held, one authored smear silhouette
shown for exactly one or two frames via `tl.set`, extreme B held. The smear shape is a
separate SVG path in the DOM from t=0 at `opacity: 0`, revealed by `tl.set` and hidden by
`tl.set`, so it is correct under seek in both directions. For a graphic (non-character)
version, the smear is the object's own path with `scaleX` overshoot along the travel
tangent plus a hard directional blur on the same two frames only.

**Measurable signature.** Exactly one or two frames in the run have a subject region
whose second-moment ratio `sx/sy` departs from the settled value by 2x or more along the
travel axis, and the frames either side are within 10% of settled. Blur, by contrast,
shows a gradual sharpness (M8) trough over 4 to 8 frames. A smear must not persist into
the settle: sharpness on the arrival frame is back to its held value.

### 13. `wireframe-state-beat` (NEW)

**What it is.** The same composition cuts to a flat grey line-art or wireframe version of
itself and back, as a rhythmic beat rather than as an opening reveal.

**Exemplar.** Fustic Studio, CoCreate MAX for Adobe MAX `[ep 10 #382-#396]`. The record's
precise reading: the greyscale-to-colour swap "is used as a beat, not as a reveal at the
start, and it comes back one element at a time rather than all at once" (at #396 the MAX
mark alone is back in colour while the rest is still grey). Second exemplar: Strange
Beast's Eaters Welcome, where the finished scene turns into a green and pink wireframe on
a blue grid `[ep 14 #370-#371]`.

**Mechanism.** Two complete layers at identical geometry: the finished art, and a
stroke-only, fill-`none` version with a single flat stroke colour. Cut between them with
`tl.set` on the same beat as an audio hit. Return one element at a time by staggering
`tl.set` calls on per-element pairs rather than crossfading the whole layer, which is
what makes it read as a build rather than as a glitch. Never crossfade; the value is in
the hardness of the swap.

**Measurable signature.** On the swap frame, the frame's colour-cluster count collapses
to two or three and the edge-pixel fraction rises (outlines everywhere, no fills). The
return is staggered: the cluster count recovers over 3 to 8 frames rather than in one.
Duration of the wireframe state is 2 to 8 frames, short enough to read as a stutter.

### 14. `scatter-resolve-sentence` (NEW; neither `waterfall-entry` nor `kinetic-beat-slam` resolves scatter into a line)

**What it is.** Words appear at scattered positions and rotations, then converge into a
readable sentence. Plus two siblings: an acronym that expands in place, and a word that
is struck through and replaced above it.

**Exemplar.** Allen Laseter, Reshaping Design `[ep 11 #317-#328]`. Single words ("our",
"ever", "industries", "and", "world", "lacking") scattered at random positions and
rotations on olive, then resolving into "our world and industries are lacking necessary
perspectives" on coral (#317-#320). "BIPOC" expanding in place to "Black IPOC", then
"Black Indigenous People of C", then "Black Indigenous People of Color" (#321-#324).
"review" struck through with a hand-drawn line and "revise" written above it in orange
(#325-#328).

**Mechanism.** Lay the sentence out first, in flow, at its final position, and measure
each word's rest offset at build time after `await document.fonts.ready`. Then `fromTo`
each word from an index-derived scatter offset (hashed, never `Math.random`) back to
`x: 0, y: 0, rotation: 0`. Because the layout is authored and the tween is an offset, the
sentence is correct at t=0 under seek and correct at the end without measurement drift.
For the acronym, the expansion letters exist in the DOM from t=0 inside per-letter
wrappers with `overflow: hidden` and `scaleX: 0` on the inner span; expanding is a
`scaleX` tween on the inner spans, never a `width` tween. For the strike-and-replace, the
line is an SVG path revealed by `stroke-dashoffset` and the replacement word rises from a
mask.

**Measurable signature.** Every word's centroid path is monotone toward its rest position
with no reversal; all words settle within a window of 0.5 s or less; at settle, every
word's baseline y is identical within 1 px, which is the test that the layout was
measured rather than typed. Total scatter distance per word scales with its distance from
the sentence's centre, not randomly.

### 15. `one-take-variable-speed-camera` (EXTENDS `multi-phase-camera`)

**What it is.** A single uncut camera move for the whole piece whose speed is the pacing
device: fast through the joins, slow over the content. There are no cuts at all.

**Exemplar.** Coat of Arms, Cloud for Arrow Electronics `[ep 02 S14-S83]`. Narration at
`S62-S72`: "the main motion throughout the whole piece is one long uncut camera movement
backwards which speeds up during transitions and slows down to show each scene. This
makes it hard to look away because there's no actual breaks, it's one scene that's
continually evolving". He calls it "the animated equivalent of the one long continuous
takes you'll recognize from some of your favorite films".

**Mechanism.** One `.world` wrapper, one writer of its transform, scenes laid out at
different `translateZ` depths so the pull-back reveals them in order. The camera pose is
a single chained sequence of `fromTo` tweens on one state object, alternating
`power2.inOut` bursts (the joins, 0.3 to 0.5 s) with near-linear dwells (the content,
1.2 to 2.5 s). Because there are no cuts, the whole piece must carry the parallax budget:
foreground 1.0x, midground 0.5x, background 0.2x, all tweened at the same position with
the same ease so they share one velocity profile. Depth of field on non-focal planes via
a `--dof` custom property tweened on the timeline, resolving to 0 on each dwell.

**Measurable signature.** Zero cut frames: no frame pair in the piece has an ink-count
change above 40%. The camera driver's speed series is bimodal, with dwell speed under
25% of burst speed and a ratio of at least 3 between them. Tracked elements from
different depth layers show displacement ratios near 0.2, 0.5 and 1.0.

**Summary of what is new.** Eleven recipes are NEW (`stepped-cadence-mix`,
`luminance-cut`, `hard-offset-shadow`, `locked-palette-contract`,
`selective-edge-texture`, `lod-by-scale`, `scale-jump-cut`, `folding-picture-plane`,
`offset-twin-delay`, `wireframe-state-beat`, `scatter-resolve-sentence`). Four EXTEND an
existing rule (`persistent-handoff-object` and `matte-card-grow` extend
`card-morph-anchor`; `authored-smear-pose` extends `motion-blur-streak`;
`one-take-variable-speed-camera` extends `multi-phase-camera`).

## Concept seeds for an original 15-second piece

Ten distinct concepts inspired by the playlist, none a copy of any piece in it. All are
sized for 640x360, 30 fps, 15 seconds, 450 frames, deterministic HTML plus GSAP on a
single paused timeline.

### 1. Seven Ways to Fold

**Dominant idea.** One mark folds into seven different things and returns to itself.

**Style family.** Swiss reductionist shape grammar.

**Emotional intent.** Dry precision with a small joke at the end, when the seventh fold
refuses to unfold.

**Rhythm.** Six fast folds on a 1.7 s grid, then one that takes twice as long and holds
for three seconds.

**What makes it read as professional.** The offset. Every fold is the same action, so the
only variation available is timing between the panels, which is exactly the value the
narrator named in Colophon: "the way these box tabs overlap and fold out in a slightly
offset manner creates a variation" `[ep 01 #51-#52]`. Two colours, one hard offset
shadow, no arcs.

**Recipes exercised.** `locked-palette-contract`, `hard-offset-shadow`,
`offset-twin-delay`, `stepped-cadence-mix` (the shadow steps on twos, the mark does not).

**Difficulty.** Low to medium. The folds are `rotateY` on grouped SVG panels with
`transform-origin` on the crease; the only real work is authoring seven silhouettes that
share a start state.

### 2. Small Hands, Big Room

**Dominant idea.** A figure crosses a room; each cut makes the room larger and the figure
smaller, until the last cut brings us back to their hands.

**Style family.** Flat-vector locked palette with painterly grain.

**Emotional intent.** Isolation resolving into resolve. Not sad; determined.

**Rhythm.** Three cuts out, each holding longer than the last, then one hard push back in
and a five-second hold.

**What makes it read as professional.** Palette and stroke weight identical across all
four framings so the jump reads as scale, and detail dropping as the camera pulls back.
Both are stated rules `[ep 09 Screens 219-234]`, `[ep 06 #313-#317]`.

**Recipes exercised.** `scale-jump-cut`, `lod-by-scale`, `locked-palette-contract`,
`selective-edge-texture`.

**Difficulty.** Medium to high. The figure is the hard part: a walk cycle from
`character-motion.md` §3, four key poses on a rig, plus three LOD versions of it. Budget
most of the build here and keep the rooms trivially simple.

### 3. Hand-off

**Dominant idea.** One sphere travels through five worlds in a single uncut pull-back,
passing through an aperture at every join.

**Style family.** Gradient field, with a 3D-feel camera.

**Emotional intent.** Absorption. The piece should be hard to look away from, which is
the exact benefit the narrator claims for the technique `[ep 02 S62-S72]`.

**Rhythm.** Continuous, with the speed bimodal: five fast joins of about 0.4 s and four
dwells of 2 to 2.5 s.

**What makes it read as professional.** No cuts at all, correct parallax ratios between
depth layers, and the sphere's own gradient fill animating throughout, which is a
specific compliment in the playlist: "even the gradients in the sphere that's the focal
point are animated" `[ep 02 S106-S112]`.

**Recipes exercised.** `one-take-variable-speed-camera`, `persistent-handoff-object`,
`matte-card-grow` (each aperture), `luminance-cut` (one join only), plus
`depth-of-field-blur` from the existing rule set.

**Difficulty.** High. Five worlds is five times the asset budget, the parallax must be
right or the whole conceit fails, and the aperture pass-throughs need the sphere clipped
by a shape it is simultaneously in front of. Cut to three worlds if time is short.

### 4. Eight Strips

**Dominant idea.** Diagonal tape strips accumulate across the frame until they cover it,
then one peels off and lets a single word through.

**Style family.** Flat-vector locked palette, editorial.

**Emotional intent.** Accumulating pressure, then one small opening. Not resolution;
relief.

**Rhythm.** Eight strips accelerating over eight seconds, two seconds of full blackout,
one slow peel, four-second hold on the word.

**What makes it read as professional.** Nothing is cleared before the next element
arrives, which is the accumulation pattern the playlist uses repeatedly
`[ep 05 #445-#447]`, `[ep 05 #108-#114]`. The peel is the only arc in the piece.

**Recipes exercised.** `locked-palette-contract`, `hard-offset-shadow`,
`scatter-resolve-sentence` (the reveal word uses the mask half of it), `luminance-cut`
(the blackout is a luminance event in the other direction).

**Difficulty.** Low. Strips are rotated divs with `clip-path` reveals; the peel is a
rotation about an off-frame origin with a two-tone underside. This is the safest of the
ten to hit reliably.

### 5. Read It Twice

**Dominant idea.** A single sentence sets itself, is struck through, and revises itself
into a better sentence.

**Style family.** Kinetic type in context.

**Emotional intent.** Craft and self-correction. Quiet, with one moment of wit.

**Rhythm.** Slow build, one strike, long hold. Roughly 6 s to set, 1 s strike, 8 s on the
revised line.

**What makes it read as professional.** The type is set with visible apparatus (a
baseline rule, a measured left margin, a caret) rather than floating, which is Oddfellows'
whole argument `[ep 03 S111-S119]`. The strike-through is drawn, not a `text-decoration`.
One typeface, extreme weight contrast, wide tracking.

**Recipes exercised.** `scatter-resolve-sentence` (all three of its forms),
`matte-card-grow`, `locked-palette-contract`.

**Difficulty.** Low to medium. The trap is layout measurement: every offset must come
from `getBoundingClientRect` after `await document.fonts.ready`, or the sentence resolves
to the wrong place on a cold load. Everything else is spans and `clip-path`.

### 6. Boil

**Dominant idea.** One shape breathes for fifteen seconds while its edge texture is
re-seeded on twos. There are no cuts and no other elements.

**Style family.** Grainy painterly, one-bit adjacent.

**Emotional intent.** Meditative. The piece should reward staring.

**Rhythm.** No cuts. One 15-second breath cycle with a slow inhale and a faster exhale,
and the texture boiling at a constant 15 fps underneath it.

**What makes it read as professional.** The texture does the motion, which is the thing
the narrator keeps naming: "texture makes the thin precise strokes of these computer
calculated paths feel so warm and uneven" `[ep 02 S184-S185]`. And it is selective: only
the lit arc of the contour boils, per Nerdo `[ep 01 #42]`.

**Recipes exercised.** `selective-edge-texture`, `stepped-cadence-mix`,
`locked-palette-contract`, plus `sine-wave-loop` from the existing rule set.

**Difficulty.** Medium. The determinism is the work: the turbulence seed must step
through an authored array indexed off quantised timeline time, never off a counter, or
the piece will not seek. Also watch the delivery encode: a 15-second single shot at low
amplitude is exactly the case `twelve-principles.md` §14.4 warns can be quantised out of
an H.264 file.

### 7. The Second Eye

**Dominant idea.** A creature with two eyes reacts to something off-frame, and one eye
is always three frames behind the other.

**Style family.** Flat-vector locked palette, character loop.

**Emotional intent.** Comedy. The joke is entirely in the offset.

**Rhythm.** Four seconds of nothing, one blink, one late blink, a look off-frame, then a
slow return and a hold. Loop-shaped: the last frame matches the first.

**What makes it read as professional.** One well-timed secondary motion carrying the whole
piece, which the narrator says outright about an ox nodding its head
`[ep 06 #447]`. The offset is the design, not a garnish.

**Recipes exercised.** `offset-twin-delay`, `authored-smear-pose` (one frame on the
head turn), `hard-offset-shadow`, `locked-palette-contract`.

**Difficulty.** Low. Two circles, two lids, one head group, three authored poses. This is
the highest ratio of read-as-professional to build-cost in the list, and the best first
build if the goal is to prove the recipe set.

### 8. Rough Cut

**Dominant idea.** The piece builds itself: blue construction lines, then clean line,
then flat fill, then final, and each pass runs faster than the last.

**Style family.** Flat-vector locked palette with a process conceit.

**Emotional intent.** Satisfaction. The pleasure of something resolving.

**Rhythm.** Three passes at 6 s, 4 s and 2 s, then a three-second hold on the finished
frame.

**What makes it read as professional.** The playlist treats the rough as content in its
own right and the narrator asks for more of it `[ep 10 #482-#486]`. The trick that makes
it work rather than read as a loading state: each pass is a complete, composed frame, not
a partial one.

**Recipes exercised.** `wireframe-state-beat`, `matte-card-grow`,
`locked-palette-contract`, `stepped-cadence-mix` (the construction pass runs on fours).

**Difficulty.** Low to medium. Three complete versions of the same artwork at identical
geometry is the cost; the timeline is trivial once they exist.

### 9. Praxis

**Dominant idea.** A zoetrope drum: a ring of cells behind a ring of slits, spinning, with
the camera pushing in until the slits fill the frame.

**Style family.** Mechanical, three-dimensional feel, one-bit adjacent.

**Emotional intent.** Hypnotic, then slightly uncomfortable as the drum speeds past the
point where the illusion holds.

**Rhythm.** Slow spin-up over 8 s, one hard acceleration, three seconds at speed, one
sudden stop and a two-second hold on a single readable cell.

**What makes it read as professional.** Committing to the mechanism. The playlist's
version of this idea, VW's The Wheel, is praised because "the devices they built and
showed on camera were really captivating and this was all done practically"
`[ep 07 #270-#276]`. The digital version cannot claim that, so its claim has to be that
the geometry is correct: the slit count, the cell count and the rotation rate must be in
the right relationship or it is just a spinning circle.

**Recipes exercised.** `stepped-cadence-mix`, `one-take-variable-speed-camera`,
`lod-by-scale`, `hard-offset-shadow`.

**Difficulty.** High, and with a specific hazard. A slitted drum spinning at speed is a
scrolling high-contrast striped pattern, which is precisely what
`twelve-principles.md` §14.3 flags under WCAG 2.3.1. Cap the effective strobe rate, keep
the slit contrast well below full, and check the delivered file, not just the frame dump.
Strobing on the leading edges is the other risk: at 640 wide, 0.5% of frame width is 3 px
per frame, and a fast drum will exceed that easily.

### 10. Gone Quiet

**Dominant idea.** A full headline holds; everything except one word dims and defocuses;
a single luminance event resolves it.

**Style family.** Grainy painterly with a locked palette, type-led.

**Emotional intent.** Deliberately quiet. One idea, held long enough to be uncomfortable,
then released.

**Rhythm.** Six-second hold, one slow four-second dim, one three-frame flash, five-second
hold on the surviving word.

**What makes it read as professional.** The confidence to hold, which is a stated virtue
`[ep 09 Screens 201-211]`, and the use of light as the event rather than as a finish
`[ep 06 #157-#163]`. The dim is a rack focus plus an opacity drop, never below 0.35
opacity, per `depth-of-field-blur`.

**Recipes exercised.** `luminance-cut`, `selective-edge-texture`,
`locked-palette-contract`, plus `depth-of-field-blur` from the existing rule set.

**Difficulty.** Low, with one caveat. The whole piece is one shot and mostly holds, so it
will fail a naive duplicate-frame check unless the ambient motion is declared. Give the
held type one bounded breathe (scale 1.000 to 1.012 over the beat) and keep the grain
boiling so no two frames are identical, then declare the intent in the manifest.

## Practical takeaways for the skill

Six things this playlist says that should change how a build is planned, in order of how
much they would improve a typical generated piece.

1. **Lock the palette before writing a single tween.** Three to five tokens, one reserved
   for a semantic job. This is the highest-leverage single decision in the whole
   document, and it costs nothing.

2. **Design the transitions before the shots.** Decide what survives each cut, or what
   grows to fill, or what hands off. If nothing does, the cut is unfinished.

3. **Use scale, not camera moves, for emphasis.** A hard cut from a wide to a macro of
   the same world, with the palette held, does more than a push-in and is cheaper and
   more deterministic.

4. **Give one layer its own cadence.** A single stepped element under a smooth camera is
   the fastest way to make a DOM composition stop looking like a DOM composition, and it
   is a technique the canon currently discourages by accident.

5. **Put the texture on the lit edges, not over the frame.** A global grain overlay is a
   delivery aid. Selective edge texture is a design decision, and the difference is
   visible.

6. **Spend the remaining budget on holds.** Almost every piece in this playlist that the
   narrator praises for pacing is built from held poses with fast transitions between
   them, not from continuous motion. Stillness after motion is what makes the motion
   land.
